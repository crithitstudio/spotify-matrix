# THE CATALOGUE — Series Lore Bible

*Internal — spoilers for the entire series. THE OBSERVER is Case One.*

## The rule under everything

There is a class of thing that exists the way a *description* exists.
Not matter, not spirit: **accumulated reference**. Every witness account,
photograph, tape, rumor, complaint letter, nightmare and glance contributes
detail. Enough detail, sustained long enough, and the description no longer
needs its referent — it stands on its own, and then it stands up.

Working name (never used in-game): **descripta**. In-world documents only
ever talk around them: "it is more there the longer I look",
"attention is a nutrient", "he is almost done".

Properties (consistent across the series, never stated outright):

1. **Attention is metabolism.** Observation adds detail; detail is mass.
   Recording instruments count as observers — better ones feed better.
2. **They are drafts.** Early forms are wrong in proportion because human
   descriptions start with silhouette and posture, not fingers and teeth.
3. **They copy the nearest complete reference.** A finished descripta is a
   *portrait*. Of someone.
4. **Unobserved, they are only probable.** They move, grow and rearrange in
   the gaps. This is why buildings that host them go wrong structurally —
   space itself is part of their draft.
5. **They cannot be starved by one person.** Closing your eyes leaves every
   photograph still developing on their behalf. Destruction of the archive
   is the only diet — and someone always keeps a copy.
6. **They want to be finished more than they want anything.**

## Case One — Halcyon Court ("THE OBSERVER")

- **1961.** Halcyon Construction breaks into a cavity under the southern
  footing ("Site 3"). Surveying it makes it more precise. Worker T. Brekke
  spends 40 minutes inside and stops being reportable. Owner A. Ferrier caps
  it and builds anyway: sixty years of tenants stacked on top of an
  unfinished description — a lens assembly focused downward.
- **1963–1988.** The complaint ledger: the figure sharpens decade by decade.
  Management stops counting in '88. The night-watch log (Oct 1987) works out
  the mechanism and requests a blind replacement.
- **Now.** The building is scheduled for demolition. The Witness is one long
  look from finished, and its scaffolding is about to be rubble. It needs a
  completed portrait to survive the demolition — a body of reference dense
  enough to stand without the building. The caretaker, alone, curious,
  equipped with a camera: a portrait sitting.
- **The ending.** "I only know what you showed me." Two identical figures on
  CAM 1. The game refuses to say which one the morning belongs to. Both
  readings must remain fully coherent:
  - *He left; it stayed and walked out second, into the world.*
  - *It left first; he is still inside when the charges go.*

## The recurring connective tissue (series constants)

- **HALCYON** — the developer/owner web (Halcyon Construction, Halcyon
  Redevelopment Corp, later Halcyon Storage & Archival). Not a conspiracy;
  a family firm that learned in 1961 that documentation is containment *and*
  feeding, and has been quietly buying archives ever since.
- **SITE numbering** — Site 3 is Halcyon Court. Sites 1 and 2 are never
  explained in Case One. (Site 1: the quarry the stone came from. Site 2:
  the surveyor's own house. Reserve for later cases.)
- **The brass key stamped S3** — physical keys stamped S# recur; Halcyon
  cuts one per site, always brass, always taped behind the last page of
  something.
- **1961 / 6:12 / 28th** — the numeric triad. Construction year; the
  timestamp on the final CCTV frame; the demolition date. They reappear as
  case numbers, frequencies, and room numbers in later cases.
- **"it learns by looking"** — the tagline is in-world graffiti, first
  scratched above the chair in 4A. It predates the game's events and will
  predate every case.
- **The Catalogue** — the series title is the threat: somewhere, the
  accumulated documentation of every case is being kept together.

## Sequel hooks (authored into Case One)

1. **The photographs survive.** The player's own exposures are real PNGs in
   their save directory. Case Two acknowledges this: photographs of a
   descripta are seed stock.
2. **R.** — the previous caretaker who "lasted four months" and wrote the
   handover note. He is alive, and he took his logbook with him.
3. **The demolition.** Rubble goes somewhere. Halcyon Storage & Archival
   accepts the site records — and the security tapes — on the 29th.
4. **The Witness's state.** Whichever reading the player carries, one thing
   is canon: *something with a finished face rode the morning out.*

## Case Two (concept): "THE ARCHIVE"

An overnight digitization contractor in Halcyon Storage & Archival's
records annex. Every tape you digitize becomes searchable; every search
ranks results; every ranking is attention. The paranoia system inverts:
the entity personalizes not to how you *watch*, but to what you *index*.
The final reel is CAM 1, 06:12, Halcyon Court — and the contractor
recognizes the man who walks out second.

## Case Three (sketch): "THE BROADCAST"

A regional TV station, 1994, re-running archival footage for an anniversary
special. A descripta that finished decades ago has been living as a face
that appears in exactly one frame of a jingle — and the special is about to
loop it into forty thousand living rooms at once.

## Ambiguity policy (series law)

- Never name the entity class in-game.
- Never show the moment of replacement, ever, in any case.
- Every supernatural event must retain one mundane reading at the moment it
  happens (fatigue, instrument error, memory), even if the sum cannot.
- The player's own recordings are always honored as diegetic objects.
- Endings differ in *implication*, never in exposition.
